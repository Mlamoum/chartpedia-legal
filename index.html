Public data, told as a chart race.

Chartpedia is a data-visualisation channel. We take public datasets — records, rankings, comparisons — and turn them into animated charts and short narrated explainers, so a number that would take a paragraph to describe takes ten seconds to see.

What we publish

Two formats, from the same research:

  • Shorts — a 30-to-60-second vertical chart race. One question, one dataset, one ranking that changes on screen. Published several times a week.
  • Documentaries — a longer landscape explainer, five to eight minutes, that takes a single record and walks through why it moved: the engineering, the economics, the caveats a bar chart hides.

Subjects so far include the world's largest economies, tech-giant revenue, social-platform growth, the tallest buildings ever built, the fastest production cars, the fastest spacecraft, Grand Slam titles, and the fastest piston-engine fighter aircraft ever flown.

How a Chartpedia video gets made

  1. Pick a question with a real answer. Something that resolves to a number and a ranking — not an opinion poll.
  2. Pull the data from a citable public source. National statistics offices, company filings, sanctioning bodies, standard reference works. The source is named on screen and in the description.
  3. Check the figures before anything is drawn. Where two authorities measure the same thing differently, we say which standard we used, and label anything that isn't like-for-like.
  4. Render the animation. Charts are generated programmatically from the checked dataset, so what you see on screen is the data — nothing is drawn by hand to look better.
  5. Narrate and publish to YouTube and Instagram.

Accuracy and corrections

Public datasets get revised, restated and corrected. A figure that was current at publication may not be current when you watch, and charts are simplified by design in order to be readable.
